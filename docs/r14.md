## 2. The items

### 43. Last call for every digital mode, and a list from every watched channel

**What exists.** Tools > Last call (`CONFIG_CALL_REPLAY`, `prepare_call_replay`): the P25 receiver's IMBE frames go
through `voice.tap = replay_tap` (`core/p25/p25_lab.c:1115`, P25 channel receive only) into `rp_buf[400][11]`
(`p25_lab.c:529-530`, 8 s, 4,400 B from the heap); "Play" decodes them again through the same IMBE decoder on the
P25 channel in use. Recent (`CONFIG_CALL_LOG`, 20 entries) already logs calls of every mode (`ui_app.c:357-560`:
P25, YSF, FM ID, NXDN, dPMR, and DMR), and the heard list (`heard.h`, 16 entries with the frequency).

**How each mode's voice reaches the vocoder** (measured in the source):

| Mode | Frames | Path | Bytes / 20 ms frame | Bytes / s |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| P25 | IMBE 4400 bit/s (88 bits of voice) | P25 worker, `voice` IMBE decoder (software, `libdm32_imbe`) | 11 | 550 |
| YSF VW | IMBE full rate | the same decoder (`voice`) | 11 | 550 |
| DMR | AMBE+2 72-bit air frames | `dmr_call` -> `ambeVoiceSink()` (`radio_sm.c:5373`) -> the stock AMBE codec in the audio task | 9 | 450 |
| NXDN | AMBE+2 | `radio_nxdn.c:159` `voice_out` -> `ambeVoiceSink()->frame` | 9 | 450 |
| dPMR, YSF DN | AMBE+2 | `p25_lab.c:423`, `:448` -> `ambeVoiceSink()` | 9 | 450 |

So **two taps cover all five**: `voice.tap` (already there) for the IMBE modes, and one wrapper around
`ambeVoiceSink()` (a `dmr_voice_sink_t` that records the 72-bit frame, then calls the real one) for every AMBE mode.
Storing the 9-byte air frame (not the 49-bit decoded one) lets a replay go back through the same `sink->frame`, the
FEC and concealment included. **Replay through the same path is possible** for AMBE in any mode: NXDN already drives
the AMBE sink from an FM-side listener at a 20 ms pace (`radio_nxdn.c:157-178`), the replay copies that (begin,
frames every 20 ms from the radio task, end). IMBE replay stays tied to the P25 worker: it plays only while a P25 (or
YSF) channel runs the worker, as today ("Play on a P25 channel" otherwise).

**Design.** One frame ring shared by all modes in the existing heap block, and a small call table:

- Ring: `rp_buf` 4,400 B as bytes; a frame entry is the codec byte (IMBE 11 / AMBE 9) and the payload. 4,400 B hold
  **~8 s of P25 / YSF VW or ~9.7 s of AMBE** in total, across calls. Each +1 KB of heap adds ~1.8 s (P25) / ~2.2 s
  (AMBE); the heap has ~3.6 KB free, so +1 KB at most without the hotspot's RAM back.
- Call table (static): K = 8 entries x 16 B = **128 B**: ring offset and frame count, mode, codec, the multi-watch
  letter (A-F, or none), source ID / callsign hash, talkgroup, the UI clock's second. An entry whose frames were
  overwritten shows in the list without "Play".
- The list: Tools > Last call becomes a list of the last N = 8 calls, "B DMR TG 2862 3101234 2m" (letter, mode, target,
  caller, age), newest first; OK plays it (the latest K that still have frames), Back stops. The multi-watch ring stops
  on a channel with activity, so calls from every watched channel arrive through the same taps: only the letter is
  new (the radio core knows which ring slot is tuned; Recent gets the same 1-byte field, +20 B).
- Replay never transmits; it is refused while transmitting and stops on PTT or on a new call (a call in progress
  always wins).

**Cost (estimate):** flash **2.5-3.5 KB** (the sink wrapper, the ring and table, the AMBE pacer, the list screen,
strings EN + TR); static RAM **~150 B**; heap unchanged (4,400 B) or +1 KB by choice. **Verdict: fits with reclaim**
(owner build) / fits (public). Risk: the AMBE replay feeds the stock codec outside a received call; NXDN shows the
pattern works, but a replay while the multi-watch ring hops must hold the ring (as a call does).

### 44. Air band: watch several frequencies, AM as a channel mode, the airfield table

**What exists.** AM is not stored in the codeplug (`core/radio/radio_am.h:3-5`): an FM channel or the VFO inside
108-137 MHz is received in AM on a radio that can (`radio_am_channel`), and never transmits there
(`radio_am_tx_refused`). The squelch works on the RSSI in AM (`RADIO_AM_SQL_REF_DBM`). Tools > Air band tunes the VFO;
Tools > Airfields lists 79 airfields with 188 frequencies and tunes the VFO on OK. Multi-watch rings 3-6 letters,
each a channel or its own VFO (`CONFIG_VFO_MODES`, AM included in the VFO's modes).

**a) Watch several air frequencies at once.** The DM-32UV has one receiver (item 46): "at once" can only be a scan
or a ring that hops. The cheapest honest form reuses the multi-watch ring: "Watch these" on an airfield's details
puts up to 6 of its frequencies on the ring's own VFO letters (AM because they are in the air band) and starts the
ring; Back / stopping the watch puts the user's ring back. Also from Tools > Air band: "Watch" with typed frequencies.
Cost **0.8-1.3 KB flash**, static RAM ~0-30 B (the saved ring is the ring's own state). To check before building: the
ring's dwell and squelch on AM (the RSSI-based squelch must work per letter; the ring today is tested with FM / DMR /
P25 letters: test_mwatch_radio.inc gets AM letters).

**b) AM as a channel mode.** Today the editor shows such a channel as FM with its TX rows. Proposal: in the channel
editor and on Home, a channel inside 108-137 MHz shows Mode "AM" (not editable), its TX rows hidden, stored as FM +
receive only, exactly what the CPS and qdmr read and write. A real `RADIO_MODE_AM` in the record would not survive the
CPS / qdmr round trip and is **not proposed**. AM outside the air band is **not available** on this radio (the maker lists AM receive for 108-136 MHz only and RadioFoundry receives AM only in 108-137 MHz,
`radio_am.h`; docs/spec/hardware-dm32.md 8.7). Cost **0.2-0.4 KB**.

**c) The airfield table.** Checked the shipped table (`integration/radiofoundry/air/airfield_data.c`):
`tools/airfields.py` drops only exact duplicates (same type **and** frequency, line 164), so **every frequency is
kept**. 11 of the 79 airfields have one service on several frequencies: LTAG Incirlik (GND x2, TWR x2, APP x2), LTAI
Antalya (TWR x2, APP x4), LTFJ Sabiha Gokcen (ATIS x2, GND x2), LTAN, LTAO, LTBJ, LTBW, LTCC, LTCT, LTFE, LTFO (TWR or
APP x2). The details page lists them all; the list line shows only the first TWR. Proposal: "TWR 118.100 +1" on the
list line. Cost ~100 B.

**Verdict:** a) and b) fit with reclaim (~1.5 KB together); c) nothing missing, a small display change.

### 45. Spectrum: a flowing 3D waterfall

**What exists.** Tools > Spectrum sweeps up to 64 points (`SPECTRUM_POINTS_MAX`); the waterfall keeps 40 rows of 64
points at 4 bits (`wfall.h`: 1,280 B from the heap while the screen is open) and draws them as coloured rows.

**Design.** A third view (`*` cycles bars / waterfall / 3D): the same rows drawn as a ridge plot in perspective, the
newest in front: row r at depth z = r, x scaled by 1 / (1 + k z) around the centre, y raised by z; each row a
polyline of its 64 levels, filled below with the background to hide the rows behind (painter's order, back to front:
no per-column horizon buffer). Colour by level through the 8 colour slots the waterfall uses (the 4 bpp panel keeps a
16-entry palette, `gfx.h`; no new colours needed). 24 of the 40 rows are enough to read; it "flows" one row per sweep.

**Cost (estimate):** flash **1.0-1.6 KB**; RAM 0 (the waterfall's heap rows); stack: a few locals (the UI task has
196 B beyond the margin: the drawing must keep its points in the heap block, not on the stack). CPU: 24 polylines x
63 segments and their fills, once per sweep (a sweep takes about a second or more): well within the UI task.
**Verdict: fits with reclaim** (owner) / fits (public).

### 46. Cross-band repeater VHF <-> UHF

**Hardware first.** The DM-32UV has **one RF transceiver** (FD6818, `hardware-dm32.md` 8: "Single-chip VHF/UHF
transceiver") with **one synthesizer** for receive and transmit (`hardware-dm32.md:781`: split channels "have one
synthesizer for both"; the radio retunes it per DMR burst). Its state register is RX (0xBFF1), TX (0xC1FE) or idle,
"Always go through idle between RX and TX" (8.3), and for transmit **both LNAs are switched off** (8.5). There is no
second receiver (the RDA5807 is a broadcast FM receiver only, 8.7). **Receiving on one band while transmitting on the
other is impossible on this radio.** A real-time FM cross-band repeater cannot be built.

**Honest alternatives:**

1. **DMR, TDMA cross-band through the SFR machinery** (near real time). The single-frequency repeater (`sfrRunner`,
   `docs/spec/sfr.md`; on air at 461.1375 MHz, `hardware-dm32.md` 8.9) receives a burst in one time slot and repeats it
   ~90 ms later in the other, retuning per burst. A cross-band variant would receive slot 1 on A (VHF) and transmit slot
   2 on B (UHF), retuning **across bands** per burst: band select (0x1A / 0x40), the LNA / PA band selects, the
   synthesizer. Unknowns: the PLL lock time after a retune ("not measured yet", 8.3) and a band change within the TDMA
   guard; the same-band SFR already takes bit errors while it switches (8.9: 212 of 216 repeated frames needed Golay
   corrections, 4 uncorrectable). DMR users only, both sides DMR direct mode. Cost ~2-3 KB flash on top of SFR, ~100 B
   RAM. **Research grade: SDR bench first; not proposed for r14.**
2. **Store-and-forward cross-band ("parrot")**: record an over on A, then transmit it on B (and B to A), the screen
   locked with a CROSS mark, offset (repeater) frequencies refused, both frequencies in the amateur bands.
   - **DMR**: the frames are already coded (450 B/s): the over goes into a frame buffer and out again as a DMR
     transmission on B. 4,400 B (the Last call ring) holds ~9.7 s: too short for real overs. A 30 s over needs
     ~13.5 KB: only the P25 worker's `voice` + `fifo` (12.7 KB) + `rp_buf` (4.4 KB) = 17.1 KB (~38 s) come near, in a
     mode that stops P25 / YSF VW / Last call while it runs; the overlay needs the quiescing docs/plus.md describes.
   - **FM**: 8 kHz PCM is 16 KB/s: impossible in RAM. The received FM audio would have to be coded on the fly with the
     AMBE encoder (the stock codec encodes for DMR TX; FM receive audio reaches the CPU at 8 kHz as APRS uses it,
     `modem-hrc7000.md` 8.5) and decoded again for FM transmit: robot-like quality, the same 17 KB for ~38 s, and a
     codec path (FM audio into the encoder) that no one has measured.
   - Cost: flash **3-4.5 KB** (mode, lock screen, recorder / player, gates, ID), RAM the shared 17 KB overlay (no new
     static RAM beyond ~100 B). **Fits only with reclaim and the P25 worker's buffers; FM variant unproven.**
   - **Legal / operational** (owner to confirm against BTK rules): an automatic relay is a repeater; unattended
     operation needs its own licence in Turkey, so this must be **attended** (the operator present, the radio on the
     screen it locked); the callsign identification on both frequencies (the CW ID machinery, `radio_cwid.c`, every
     10 min and at the end); a transmit time-out per over (the radio's TOT, at most 3 min) and a duty limit; never on
     repeater inputs or outputs (the offset check), never outside the amateur bands, never on APRS / satellite
     frequencies.

**Excluded (owner, phase 2):** PMR446 (446.000-446.200 MHz) is refused for cross-band: PMR446 rules forbid
repeaters and this amateur radio is not PMR446 type-approved; dPMR is left out because this firmware has no dPMR
transmit. Cross-band runs FM, DMR and P25 on amateur bands only.

**Verdict: real-time cross-band impossible.** Store-and-forward DMR is feasible at a real cost (the P25 buffers);
FM store-and-forward is a research item.

### 47. APRS Mic-E position transmit

**What exists.** The Mic-E encoder is built (`core/aprs/aprs_plus.c:25` `aprs_mice_build`: destination with
latitude and message bits, information field with longitude, speed and course, comment) and used by APRS SOS for the
Mic-E Emergency report every 2 minutes. Normal beacons go out in the uncompressed position format (`aprs_beacon.c`).

**Design.** Tools > APRS (or Settings > APRS): "Beacon format: Standard / Mic-E" and "Mic-E message: Off duty / En
route / In service / Returning / Committed / Special / Priority" (M0-M6; Emergency stays SOS's). The beacon builder
calls `aprs_mice_build` with the same position, speed / course and comment. Shorter packets on the air (about 30 %
less air time with speed and course).

**Cost:** flash **0.4-0.7 KB**, static RAM 0 (two values in the UI settings store). **Verdict: fits with reclaim**
(a small one).

### 49. APRS heard stations: list, details, Go-To, Map

**What exists.** Tools > APRS > Heard (`ui_aprs.c:461-500`): 12 stations (`APRS_STATIONS`), call and age; OK follows
the station in Go-To (`CONFIG_NAV_FOLLOW`, "find my friend"). The station record keeps position, symbol, course,
speed, the type of the last packet, a 24-character comment and altitude (`aprs.h:131-145`). Tools > Map draws the
heard stations; "0" picks the nearest, OK shows its details there and OK again writes a message to it
(`ui_map.c:9-11`). The inbox keeps received messages (`aprs_msg_t`).

**Missing:** a details page from the list, the station's last messages, "show on Map" centred on it.

**Design.** OK on a heard station opens its page: symbol (as text: "car", "house", ...), distance and bearing from us,
position (degrees and Maidenhead), speed / course, altitude, comment, heard direct / via digipeater, how often; its
last messages from the inbox (filtered by callsign, no copy); actions: Go-To (follow, as today), Show on Map (opens
the map centred on it with the pick set; a small "centre on" entry into `ui_map.c`), Message (CONFIG_APRS_MSG).

**Cost:** flash **1.5-2.5 KB** (the page and the map entry; the symbol names table is the largest part, ~0.4 KB for
the common 20); static RAM ~4 B. **Verdict: fits with reclaim.**

## 5. What does not fit, or is impossible

- **Real-time cross-band repeat (FM or DMR as a duplex repeater): impossible** - one transceiver, one synthesizer, the
  LNAs off while transmitting.
- **FM store-and-forward at a useful length in RAM: not possible as PCM** (16 KB/s); only through the AMBE codec at
  robot-like quality and with the P25 worker's buffers; unmeasured codec path.
- **AM outside 108-137 MHz: not available** on this radio (the maker's AM range, never tried elsewhere); an explicit AM mode in the codeplug record would not survive
  the CPS / qdmr round trip (not proposed).
- **"Several air frequencies at once"** can only hop (one receiver): a call on one letter hides the others while it
  lasts.
- **Long replays** (minutes) do not fit: ~8-10 s in total with today's 4.4 KB, +2 s per extra KB of heap.

## 7. User data packs on the data chip (design only; the owner approves before anything writes there)

**Why.** The owner's radio runs the public build (no ID database in program flash) and the airfield table should be
refreshable from the official publication without us distributing it. A **user data pack** is data the user builds
from sources they download themselves (RadioID.net's user list as today; the aerodrome frequencies from DHMI's own
eAIP pages), kept on the 16 MiB data chip, never in our repository, release or firmware. It **touches hard rule 2 of
AGENTS.md** ("never touch ... the 16 MiB data chip"): building it needs the owner to amend that rule for the pack
region only, with the firmware as the only writer.

### 7.1 Principles (owner, 2026-11-20)

1. **No pack, no change:** the radio always works with its built-in tables (the cleaned airfield table; in the
   public build no ID names except the codeplug's contacts and any database the CPS already put on the chip).
2. **A PC is optional:** the PC programmer is one way to load a pack; another radio is the other (7.6).
3. **Atomic:** a pack is staged into a second slot and switched only when complete and verified; a power cut, a stop or
   a bad CRC leaves the previous pack (or none) in force.

### 7.2 Where on the data chip

RadioFoundry's map of the data chip (DM-32, `docs/spec/hardware-dm32.md` "Data flash" and "17", `hal_flash_map_t`;
writes below 0x700000 are refused by the driver and stay so):

| Area | Use today |
|---|---|
| 0x000000-0x6FFFFF | the stock firmware's and the factory's (calibration, its codeplug, its data): never touched |
| 0x700000-0x70FFFF | open-firmware EEPROM image |
| 0x710000 | a decoded calibration copy (read only fallback) |
| 0x711000-0x718FFF | settings ring (8 sectors) |
| 0x719000-0x71AFFF | journal (2 sectors) |
| 0x71B000-0x71FFFF | not used (5 sectors), but next to the calibration copy and the settings: **not proposed** |
| 0x720000-0xEFFFFF | open-firmware flash image: the CPS codeplug records, voice prompts, and the **CPS-written DMR ID database** (flash-image 0x50000 / area 2 at 0xD8000, physical 0x770000 / 0x7F8000 by the STM32 mapping, `open-firmware-cps.md` 7.4-7.5; formats N and C, read by `core/codeplug/dmr_iddb.c`, which the Plus build has) |
| 0xF00000-0xFFFFFF | native codeplug (1 MB) |

Proposed **pack region: 0xE70000-0xEFFFFF (576 KB)**, the top of the open-firmware flash image, farthest from the
calibration copy, the settings and both codeplugs: two RadioID slots of 256 KB (0xE70000, 0xEB0000) and two air band
slots of 16 KB (0xEF0000, 0xEF4000), the rest spare. To verify before building: that nothing of the open-firmware
image reaches it (only an ID database of more than ~7 MB in area 2 would; the CPS path then refuses a database that
would, and the pack header claims the region), read on the owner's backup copy (read only, nothing leaves the PC).

Note: upstream's **CPS-written ID database already works on the data chip today** (qdmr or the web CPS through the
firmware's CPS protocol, `open-firmware-cps.md` 7.5, format C `iddb-compact.md`); the Plus lookup asks it after the
built-in table (`integration/radiofoundry/iddb/iddb.h`). The pack adds what that path lacks: a version and date the
user sees, a CRC, the atomic two-slot switch, the air band data, and radio-to-radio transfer.

### 7.3 Format

A slot = a 64-byte header + the payload; the header is written **last** (the commit):

| Offset | Size | Field |
|---|---:|---|
| 0 | 4 | magic `DPK1` |
| 4 | 1 | type: 1 RadioID (payload: format C, `iddb-compact.md`), 2 air band |
| 5 | 1 | format version of the payload (1) |
| 6 | 2 | flags (reserved 0) |
| 8 | 4 | sequence (the higher valid one of the type's two slots is in force) |
| 12 | 4 | payload length |
| 16 | 4 | CRC-32 of the payload (`storage_crc32`) |
| 20 | 8 | source date, ASCII `YYYYMMDD` (RadioID's download date; the AIP's AIRAC date) |
| 28 | 16 | name, ASCII ("RadioID TR", "TR aerodromes") |
| 44 | 16 | the builder's tag (program version) |
| 60 | 4 | CRC-32 of bytes 0-59 |

Air band payload (version 1): the built-in table's own layout (`airfield_data.h`: per aerodrome ICAO, name ISO-8859-9,
lat / lon e5, kind, first, count; per frequency the channel and service type), ~6 KB for Turkey. Merge rule in the
airfield screens: an aerodrome in the pack replaces the built-in one's frequency list (same ICAO), new ICAOs are added,
the order and the "nearest first" walk are unchanged.

### 7.4 Firmware behaviour

| State | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| no valid slot (erased, wrong magic, a header or payload CRC wrong) | the built-in tables; Settings > About shows "Data pack: none" (or "invalid" with the reason) |
| one valid slot | used; About shows name, date, size |
| both valid | the higher sequence |
| a write under way (the inactive slot being staged) | the active slot stays in force; the new one counts only once its header is written |

Reads: the payload's CRC is checked once at boot (and after a pack arrives; ~0.1 s for 256 KB on SPI), then lookups
read the slot directly (no RAM copy: the ID lookup's binary search as `dmr_iddb.c` does, the airfield walk as today).

### 7.5 From the PC (optional)

The PC programmer (`pcapp/`) builds a pack **on the user's PC** from the files the user downloaded (RadioID.net's
user.csv; the DHMI eAIP AD 2.18 pages the user saved) and sends it **through the firmware's own CPS / debug-link
protocol**: a "pack write" command that the firmware turns into erase + program of the inactive slot through its
storage layer, a read-back CRC check, then the header. Our flash tools (`tools/install_*.py`) never write the data
chip, as now. Nothing derived from the AIP or RadioID is ever committed or published.

### 7.6 From another radio: DOTA content type "data pack"

A radio that holds a valid pack can send it with DOTA Transfer: a new content type next to the channel kinds.

- Pairing, code, ARQ, frame CRC-16, the order-free CRC-32 and the atomic commit as for channels (docs/dota.md 5-10).
  The manifest names the pack: type, name, source date, payload length, payload CRC-32, sequence.
- **The receiver confirms first:** on HELLO its screen shows "Data pack: RadioID TR, 2026-11-17, 98 KB. Accept?"
  (OK yes / Back no); only Yes answers HELLO_ACK. The receiver arms for packs with Role Receive, What "Data pack".
- Frames are staged by the storage job straight into the **inactive slot** (4 KB sectors erased ahead of the data), the
  payload CRC-32 is checked at END, then the header is written: "Imported data pack". Any failure leaves the active
  slot in force.
- Time (DOTA's steady rates, docs/dota.md 8: FM ~93 B/s, DMR ~124 B/s; FM adds ~8 % for the 5 s rest per keyed
  minute; estimates):

| Pack | Size | FM | DMR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Air band (Turkey) | ~6 KB | ~1 min 10 s | ~50 s |
| RadioID, Turkey and neighbours (format C) | ~78-100 KB | ~15-20 min | ~11-14 min |

  RadioID over the air is long: both radios transmit in turns for 10-20 minutes (battery, heat, the band's other
  users); the PC path is the better one for it, DOTA the fallback without a PC.
